\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}

This chapter synthesizes the experimental findings rather than repeating the numerical analysis from Chapter 5. The conclusions distinguish improvement over BM25, incremental value beyond an existing hybrid, and value of fine-tuning embedding models to overall benchmark.

\section{Summary of Findings}

\subsection{BCA Dataset Construction and Coverage}

The BCA collection contains 652 public question-answer records, of which 546 have extracted legal references and are retained for evaluation against 14,789 indexed articles. Among the retained queries, 211 have all targets available, 229 have some targets available, and 106 have none available (Table~\ref{tab:bca-collection}). 

\subsection{Hybrid Retrieval Compared with BM25}

Both BM25+BGE-M3 and BM25+BGE-M3+E5 have higher NDCG@10 than BM25 on the ALQAC and Zalo datasets and the full 546-query BCA collection (Tables~\ref{tab:5.2} and~\ref{tab:5.5}). BGE-M3 also has higher NDCG@10 than BM25 as an individual retriever on all three collections. The observed hybrid gains support combining lexical and dense scores will most likely improve retrieval performance. 

\subsection{Contribution of E5}

Adding E5 to BM25+BGE-M3 changes NDCG@10 by approximately -0.0021 on ALQAC and +0.0013 on Zalo. Neither reported query-level test provides sufficient evidence of an improvement (Table~\ref{tab:5.4}). For the full BCA collection, NDCG@10 increases from 0.2283 to 0.2297 and Hit@10 increases, while MRR@10 and MAP@10 decrease (Table~\ref{tab:5.5}). These mixed results suggest that E5 adding second to none improment to the hybrid system with BM25+BGE-M3.

\subsection{BGE-M3 Fine-Tuning}

The thesis evaluates two ways of selecting a fine-tuned BGE-M3 model: using hybrid retrieval performance or using BGE-M3's standalone retrieval performance. In both experiments, the hybrid comparison replaces pretrained BGE-M3 with its fine-tuned version while keeping BM25, E5, and the combination weights fixed.


When model selection uses standalone performance, fine-tuning increases BGE-M3's mean NDCG@10 by 0.0189, although Recall@100 decreases by 0.0091 (Table~\ref{tab:5.12}). The hybrid results show a decrease of 0.0043 for BM25+BGE-M3 and an increase of 0.0009 for BM25+BGE-M3+E5. Neither meets the predefined NDCG@10 improvement threshold of 0.01 (Table~\ref{tab:5.13}).

Therefore, the thesis concludes that BGE-ME can be finetuned to improve its own retrieval performance on Vietnamese legal data,but not necessarily in the hybrid setting.

\subsection{Creation and Evaluation of BCA Dataset}

The thesis constructs the BCA dataset from public-service questions and published answers. The benchmark performance evaluation shows that BCA's dataset or people daily questions can be used to evaluate retrieval performance but with some limitations. Additional processing is needed to improve the quality of retrieved results, including coverage gaps and other query level processing techniques.

\section{Research Contributions}

The first contribution is an empirical evaluation of hybrid retrieval for Vietnamese legal articles on the ALQAC, Zalo, and BCA datasets. The thesis compares BM25+BGE-M3 and BM25+BGE-M3+E5 with BM25 using the same queries, indexed articles, and relevance targets within each evaluation condition. Comparing the two hybrids separately identifies the observed contribution of E5. (Sections~\ref{sec:bca-results} and~\ref{sec:overall-benchmark}).

The second contribution is an evaluation of fine-tuning the BGE-M3 embedding model for standalone and hybrid retrieval. Two experiments select the model using hybrid retrieval performance and standalone dense retrieval performance on development data. The hybrid comparisons keep BM25, E5, and the combination weights fixed when replacing pretrained BGE-M3 with its fine-tuned version. This evaluation tests whether fine-tuning improves BGE-M3 on its own and whether the selected model improves the complete hybrid system (Section~\ref{sec:finetuning-results}).

The third contribution is the construction and evaluation of the BCA dataset from public-service questions and published answers. The thesis documents the collection, cleaning, reference extraction, and identifier mapping procedures used to turn these records into retrieval queries and relevance targets (Section~\ref{sec:bca-construction}). Its evaluation examines target availability in the corpus and accounts for links between questions that share legal references. This provides a public-service dataset alongside the competition benchmarks and a basis for distinguishing corpus-coverage and mapping problems from ranking errors (Section~\ref{sec:bca-results}).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Both hybrids improve NDCG@10 over BM25 on the evaluated datasets, but the contribution of E5 varies by dataset and metric. Its contribution must therefore be assessed by comparing BM25+BGE-M3+E5 directly with BM25+BGE-M3. The results do not establish one configuration as the best choice across datasets.

The fine-tuning comparisons keep BM25, E5, and fusion weights fixed when replacing pretrained BGE-M3. Their findings apply to these fixed configurations. Selecting new fusion weights after fine-tuning may change the results, but this was not evaluated. The two experiments also use different training procedures and selection criteria, so their differences cannot be attributed to one parameter. Transfer of the fine-tuned models to ALQAC or BCA was not evaluated (Section~\ref{sec:finetuning-results}).

BCA's relevance targets are extracted from published answers and may omit relevant articles or contain extraction and mapping errors. Articles absent from the corpus cannot be retrieved, while incorrect identifier mappings can prevent relevant results from receiving credit. Unavailable targets remain in the evaluation, so BCA scores reflect corpus coverage and target matching as well as ranking performance. The experiments do not isolate how much each factor contributes to the scores (Section~\ref{sec:bca-results}).

The evaluation covers Vietnamese legal article retrieval using recorded relevance targets. Measure answer correctness, citation support, the current applicability of legal provisions, or user satisfaction is out of scope. Latency, throughput, and deployment cost were also not measured. These outcomes require separate evaluation before drawing conclusions about a complete legal question-answering system.

\section{Future Work}

A new, prospectively controlled evaluation should be conducted on an untouched query set. Corpus construction, model selection, fusion configuration, and statistical procedures should be fixed before examining the final test results.

Corpus quality should also be improved, particularly for the BCA collection. Coverage gaps can be addressed through version-aware mapping of legal documents, while query-level failures can be systematically sampled and annotated using a defined rubric. This would help distinguish missing evidence, annotation limitations, lexical mismatch, semantic confusion, and fusion-related ranking errors.

Future work should further extend the evaluation from retrieval performance to the complete RAG system. This includes assessing answer correctness, citation support, legal sufficiency, latency, throughput, and computational cost. Retrieval metrics remain useful indicators, but they cannot replace direct evaluation of generated legal answers and deployed system performance.

\section{Closing Remarks}

This thesis shows that Vietnamese legal retrieval benefits from lexical–semantic hybridization, but also that more components or better dense-only scores do not automatically produce a better complete system. The most important lesson is methodological: legal retrieval claims must be tied to the exact corpus, target coverage, selection boundary, and system level being evaluated. A disciplined negative result can be as informative as a positive result when it prevents an unsupported deployment claim.

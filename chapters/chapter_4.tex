\chapter{Proposed Methodology}

This chapter defines the retrieval task, datasets, scoring methods, fusion procedure, evaluation design, and legal-domain adaptation studies. The method uses established retrieval models and a controlled evaluation pipeline; the contribution lies in the dataset construction, experimental comparison, and interpretation of results rather than in a new ranking algorithm.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let \(D=\{d_1,\ldots,d_n\}\) denote an ordered corpus of legal retrieval units. Each unit contains article text and a normalized legal identifier. For a query \(q\), the relevance set \(R_q\) contains the law--article targets recorded for that question. A retrieval configuration \(\theta\) produces a ranked list
\[
\pi_q(\theta)=(d_{(1)},d_{(2)},\ldots,d_{(k)}),
\]
where \(k=10\) for the primary evaluation window.

The selection objective is mean NDCG@10 on a designated selection population:
\[
\theta^*=\arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta}\frac{1}{|Q_{\mathrm{sel}}|}\sum_{q\in Q_{\mathrm{sel}}}\mathrm{NDCG}@10(\pi_q(\theta),R_q).
\]
The candidate set \(\Theta\) contains tested fusion weights and, in the adaptation studies, selected encoder-training recipes. Labels are used only for selection and evaluation. At retrieval time, the system receives the query text and the indexed corpus; it does not receive the answer, target labels, or relevance judgments.

This formulation makes two limitations explicit. First, a target absent from the searchable corpus cannot be retrieved by any ranking model. Second, retrieval effectiveness is not the same as generated legal-answer correctness. The study therefore evaluates article ranking, not downstream legal advice.

\section{Retrieval and Evaluation Pipeline}

The baseline architecture compares individual retrievers and weighted hybrids. BM25 provides the lexical score. BGE-M3 and multilingual-E5-base provide dense vector scores. The two-way hybrid combines BM25 and BGE-M3; the three-way hybrid combines BM25, BGE-M3, and E5.

Each component scores the same ordered corpus. Scores are normalized per query and combined by weighted addition. The resulting top-ten rankings are evaluated against the recorded targets. The system does not use a cross-encoder reranker, query router, query expansion module, approximate nearest-neighbor filter, or answer-generation stage. This restriction keeps the experiment focused on first-stage lexical–semantic retrieval.

The resulting collection contains 652 source records, of which 546 are retained for retrieval evaluation because they have at least one extracted law reference. Later coverage analysis distinguishes answerable, partially answerable, and unavailable target cases.

\subsection{BCA Target Construction}

The BCA construction process converts public question-answer records into a retrieval dataset. Question text is cleaned and retained as the query. Answer text is used only to extract law and article references; it is not appended to the query at retrieval time. Extracted law names are normalized and aligned to legal identifiers in the searchable corpus. Article identifiers are normalized separately so that matching remains identity-based rather than text-similarity-based.

Figure~\ref{fig:4.2} summarizes the construction process. The process is useful because it produces naturally occurring legal information needs outside the competition collections. Its limitations are equally important: published answers are not exhaustive relevance judgments, citation extraction is heuristic, and some cited material is absent from the indexed corpus.

\section{Lexical and Dense Representations}

BM25 scores are computed from tokenized query and article text. The lexical preprocessing lowercases text, removes non-word punctuation as token separators, and splits on whitespace. This simple policy is treated as part of the fixed lexical baseline. The experiments do not claim to evaluate every possible Vietnamese lexical analyzer or tokenization strategy.

Dense scores are produced by pretrained BGE-M3 and multilingual-E5-base encoders. Query and article embeddings are L2-normalized, and dense similarity is the vector dot product:
\[
s_{\mathrm{dense}}(q,d)=\frac{\mathbf{u}_q^\top\mathbf{v}_d}{\max(\lVert\mathbf{u}_q\rVert_2,10^{-10})\max(\lVert\mathbf{v}_d\rVert_2,10^{-10})}.
\]

The evaluation uses exhaustive corpus scoring rather than an approximate nearest-neighbor index. Therefore, a mapped and available target outside the top ten reflects scoring and fusion behavior, not candidate-generation recall. This design simplifies scientific interpretation, although it does not establish production latency.

\section{Score Normalization and Fusion}

BM25 and dense scores have different numeric scales, so they are normalized before fusion. For retriever \(r\), raw score \(s_r(q,d)\), and corpus \(C\), the implemented per-query min--max normalization is
\[
\widetilde{s}_r(q,d)=\frac{s_r(q,d)-\min_{d'\in C}s_r(q,d')}{\max\left(\max_{d'\in C}s_r(q,d')-\min_{d'\in C}s_r(q,d'),\epsilon\right)}.
\]
The small constant \(\epsilon\) prevents division by zero. Normalization is applied independently for each query and channel over the full corpus.

The two-way hybrid score is
\[
S_2(q,d)=w_b\widetilde{s}_{b}(q,d)+(1-w_b)\widetilde{s}_{g}(q,d),
\]
where \(b\) denotes BM25 and \(g\) denotes BGE-M3. The three-way score is
\[
S_3(q,d)=w_b\widetilde{s}_{b}(q,d)+w_g\widetilde{s}_{g}(q,d)+w_e\widetilde{s}_{e}(q,d),\quad w_b+w_g+w_e=1,
\]
where \(e\) denotes E5. Weights are nonnegative. The evaluated grid uses increments of 0.1. Thus, the two-way grid contains 11 candidate settings and the three-way simplex contains 66 candidate settings, including zero-weight boundaries.

For each split, training-side mean NDCG@10 selects the fusion weights, which are then fixed for evaluation on unseen data. Because the three-way grid contains the two-way boundary, a higher training-side fit does not by itself prove a benefit on unseen data. The two-way versus three-way comparison on unseen data in Chapter 5 is therefore the relevant evidence for E5's incremental value.

\section{Evaluation Design}

The baseline evaluation uses law-component grouping to reduce leakage from shared reference laws. Query groups are assigned to folds so that reference-law components are not split between training-side fusion selection and evaluation on unseen data. The resulting out-of-fold predictions measure a selection procedure rather than one universal global weight vector.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Grouped selection of fusion weights and out-of-fold evaluation}
\label{alg:grouped-fusion-selection}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Ordered queries $Q$, corpus $D$, aligned score matrices, grouped folds, candidate weight grid $W$
\Ensure Selected weights per fold and query metrics on unseen data
\For{each fold $f$}
    \State Let $T$ be the training-side query indices and $V$ the unseen-data indices
    \For{each candidate weight vector $w\in W$}
        \State Rank $D$ for every query in $T$ using the weighted score sum
        \State Compute mean query NDCG@10
    \EndFor
    \State Select the first candidate with the highest training-side mean
    \State Rank all queries in $V$ using the selected weights
    \State Store query-level metrics on unseen data
\EndFor
\State Aggregate unseen-data query rows across folds
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Article ranking with a locked hybrid configuration}
\label{alg:locked-hybrid-ranking}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Query $q$, corpus $D$, retriever scores, locked fusion weights $w$
\Ensure Ten highest-ranked articles
\State Normalize each retriever's scores over the full corpus for $q$
\For{each article $d\in D$}
    \State $\mathrm{combined}[d]\gets\sum_r w_r\,\mathrm{normalized\_score}[r,d]$
\EndFor
\State \Return the ten articles with highest combined score
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

For BCA, ordinary query-level evaluation would overstate reliability because many questions are connected through shared legal targets. The corrected protocol distinguishes a giant-component holdout, small-component out-of-fold evaluation, and a combined descriptive view. Chapter 5 reports all three because they answer different questions: performance on the dominant connected component, performance across smaller components, and descriptive behavior over the entire retained BCA collection.

\section{Legal-Domain Fine-Tuning}

Fine-tuning tests whether legal-domain supervision improves BGE-M3. The adaptation studies change only the BGE-M3 dense component. BM25 and E5 remain fixed. The primary hybrid evaluation then replaces pretrained BGE-M3 with the adapted BGE-M3 while keeping the relevant fusion policy explicit. This thesis refers to the two experiments as Study A: Hybrid-Oriented Adaptation and Study B: Dense-Oriented Adaptation.

\subsection{Training Pairs and Negatives}

Study A: Hybrid-Oriented Adaptation uses Zalo query--article pairs. For each eligible query, the training builder selects a known relevant article available in both clean and parsed views, creating paired training examples from the two corpus representations. Negative passages are mined from lexical and dense rankings after excluding known positives, duplicates, unavailable documents, and laws reserved for evaluation on unseen data.

\subsection{Training Objective and Configuration}

Study A trains BGE-M3 with a single-positive in-batch contrastive objective. Each query group contains one selected positive and seven explicit negatives. With batch size \(B\), group size \(G=8\), positive passage index \(y_i=iG\), normalized query vector \(\mathbf q_i\), normalized passage vector \(\mathbf p_j\), and temperature \(\tau=0.02\), the implemented objective is
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{batch}}=-\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=0}^{B-1}
\log\frac{\exp(\mathbf q_i^\top\mathbf p_{y_i}/\tau)}
{\sum_{j=0}^{BG-1}\exp(\mathbf q_i^\top\mathbf p_j/\tau)}.
\]

\subsection{Study B: Dense-Oriented Adaptation}

Study B evaluates BGE-M3 as a dense retriever before integrating it into hybrids. This study uses a separate recipe with an all-positive contrastive mask, five explicit negatives, a larger true contrastive batch requirement, and a Zalo-only first passing data stage. Its selected setting is evaluated with three final seeds on the same 640 official queries used for the dense and fusion comparisons.

For logits \(z_{ij}=s(q_i,d_j)/\tau\), positive candidate positions \(P_i\), candidate positions \(C_i\), and batch size \(B\), the multi-positive loss is
\[
\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}\left[\log\sum_{j\in P_i}\exp(z_{ij})-\log\sum_{j\in C_i}\exp(z_{ij})\right].
\]
Study B is reported separately from Study A because it uses a different recipe and answers a different question: whether dense-only gains transfer into complete hybrids.

\section{Method Summary}

The methodology fixes the retrieval task at article ranking, preserves query and corpus identities, compares lexical and dense signals under selected fusion weights, and evaluates legal-domain adaptation only after explicit selection boundaries. BCA adds a dataset-construction and evaluation-design contribution by exposing coverage and component-dependence limits. Chapter 5 reports the resulting effectiveness, failure analysis, and adaptation outcomes.

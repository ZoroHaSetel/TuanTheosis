\chapter{Research Methodology}

This chapter describes the research design, datasets, and implementation used to evaluate Vietnamese legal article retrieval. It presents corpus preparation and BCA dataset construction before the retrieval pipeline, score fusion, and BGE-M3 fine-tuning procedures. It then specifies the evaluation design, statistical analysis, experimental environment, and data-use limitations.

\section{Research Design and Retrieval Task}
\label{sec:research-design}

This thesis uses controlled retrieval experiments to compare lexical retrieval, dense retrieval, and their combination for Vietnamese legal questions. The system receives a question, scores the indexed legal articles, and returns a ranked list. Each article has a legal document identifier and an article identifier. The primary outcome is normalized discounted cumulative gain at rank ten (NDCG@10); the matching rules and evaluation metrics are defined in Section~\ref{sec:target-metrics}.

BM25 provides the baseline for retrieval effectiveness. Comparing BM25+BGE-M3 with BM25 assesses the benefit of combining lexical and dense retrieval. Comparing BM25+BGE-M3+E5 with BM25+BGE-M3 assesses the additional benefit of multilingual-E5-base. Dataset and corpus conditions provide separate settings for these comparisons, with the same query population retained within each comparison of corpus versions. The evaluation also distinguishes articles absent from the corpus from errors in mapping target identifiers, since both can affect measured performance.

The fine-tuning experiments compare pretrained and fine-tuned BGE-M3 in standalone dense retrieval and in the hybrid configurations. One experiment selects the model using hybrid retrieval performance on development data; the other uses standalone dense retrieval performance. The hybrid comparisons keep BM25, E5, and the combination weights fixed while replacing BGE-M3 with its fine-tuned version. This design tests whether improvements in standalone retrieval are retained after score fusion. Section~\ref{sec:bge-finetuning} describes the training procedures, and Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-design} specifies the evaluation conditions and their limitations.

Relevance labels support training, configuration selection, and evaluation. At retrieval time, the system uses only the question text and the indexed corpus. Published answers and recorded relevance targets are not retrieval inputs. The experiments therefore measure recovery of annotated legal articles rather than the correctness of a generated legal answer.

\subsection{ALQAC and Zalo Datasets}

ALQAC and Zalo provide Vietnamese legal questions with annotations identifying the relevant legal articles. The questions serve as retrieval queries, and the annotated references provide the targets used to evaluate the ranked results.

For ALQAC, the baseline evaluation combines the supplied training and private-test questions, giving 811 queries. Its reference corpus contains 3,363 articles.

For Zalo, the baseline evaluation uses 3,196 queries and a reference corpus of 61,425 articles. Each query retains its supplied relevance targets.

For both datasets, questions linked by shared legal references are kept in the same group. Fusion weights are selected using other groups before evaluation on the excluded group, as described in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-design}. All baseline methods within each dataset use the supplied reference corpus and annotations. The following subsection distinguishes this corpus from the preparation used in the completed fine-tuning experiments.

\subsection{Article Extraction and Identifier Alignment}
\label{sec:article-extraction}

Each article is stored with its text, document identifier, and article identifier. The ALQAC and Zalo benchmark uses the text supplied with each dataset.

For the Zalo fine-tuning comparisons, article text is extracted from legal documents collected in raw HTML format, then cleaned and processed. The text is split into articles and matched to the supplied records by document and article identifiers. Extracted text replaces the supplied text when a match is found and the cleaned content is nonempty. Otherwise, the supplied text is retained. This reference fallback preserves all 61,425 articles for the pretrained and fine-tuned comparisons.

Identifiers are trimmed and converted to lowercase before target matching. All retrievers use the same article order so that their scores remain linked to the correct articles.

\section{BCA Dataset Construction}
\label{sec:bca-construction}

The BCA dataset is constructed from public questions and published answers in the question-and-answer section of the Bộ Công an portal.\footnote{\url{https://bocongan.gov.vn/hoi-dap/}} Each question becomes a retrieval query, and legal references extracted from its published answer become the recorded relevance targets. Construction consists of collecting the source records, cleaning their text, extracting legal references, and mapping those references to corpus identifiers. The answer text is used to construct targets and is not supplied to the retriever.

\subsection{Motivation and Research Value}

The first purpose is to extend evaluation to questions submitted through a public-service channel. Competition benchmarks provide defined tasks and evaluation conditions; for example, ALQAC evaluates legal retrieval and question answering using a manually annotated dataset~\cite{alqac2024}. BCA adds questions concerning residence, identity documents, fire prevention, traffic penalties, and immigration. Citizens may describe their circumstances in everyday language rather than use the terms found in legislation. This motivates examining retrieval on public-service questions alongside the ALQAC and Zalo benchmark comparisons.

The second purpose is to construct a research resource beyond the supplied benchmarks. The collection and reference-extraction pipeline converts published question-answer records into queries and traceable relevance targets. It supports evaluation of BM25, BGE-M3, E5, and their combinations on a separately collected set of questions. The resulting analysis examines performance across collections, including differences in target availability and shared legal references. The resource contribution is the constructed collection and its documented processing procedure.

\subsection{Data Sources and Record Selection}

The collection script requests successive pages of question listings through the portal's application programming interface, then retrieves each question and its published answer. Each record is assigned an identifier derived from the source question identifier. During a repeated collection, records with existing identifiers are skipped to prevent duplication.

Acquisition requires separate listing and detail requests: the listing identifies available questions, while each detail response supplies the question and answer fields. Failed listing requests can interrupt discovery, and failed detail requests can leave individual records uncollected.

The saved collection contains 652 records with distinct question identifiers. Of these, 546 are retained for retrieval evaluation because they contain at least one extracted legal document reference.


\subsection{Text Cleaning and Reference Extraction}

Preprocessing covers the collected question-answer records, extracted legal articles, and inputs to each retriever. Legal references are then derived from the published answers to construct evaluation targets. The question text remains the retrieval query throughout this process.

\paragraph{Text cleaning and retriever inputs.}
The preprocessing steps are organized by their role:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cleaning question-answer records.} The response fields \texttt{content} and \texttt{answer} contain the question and published answer. Regular expressions replace HTML tags with spaces, remove leading and trailing whitespace, and reduce repeated spaces, tabs, and line breaks to a single space. Unicode Normalization Form C (NFC) standardizes equivalent character sequences in Vietnamese text. The cleaned question is used as the retrieval query, while the cleaned answer is used for reference extraction.

    \item \textbf{Extracting legal articles.} Legal HTML is converted to text lines using BeautifulSoup. Article boundaries are identified from lines beginning with ``Điều'' and its alternative spelling, followed by an article number and an optional letter suffix. The leading article marker and number are removed where present.

    \item \textbf{Preparing BM25 tokens.} Question and article text is converted to lowercase. Punctuation and other characters outside the word-character and whitespace classes are replaced with spaces, and the text is split on whitespace. Vietnamese letters with diacritics are retained. This procedure uses no external Vietnamese word-segmentation library.

    \item \textbf{Formatting dense inputs.} BGE-M3 receives question and article text without additional prefixes. Multilingual-E5-base receives the prefix \texttt{query: } for questions and \texttt{passage: } for articles. Both models use a maximum input length of 512 tokens in the baseline experiments. Section~\ref{sec:retrieval-implementation} describes the retrieval models, and Section~\ref{sec:bge-finetuning} specifies the separate fine-tuning configurations.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Legal reference extraction.}
The thesis processes the published answer using the following steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Identifying articles and documents.} The answer is divided into spans using punctuation and line boundaries. Within each span, the extractor identifies article numbers, including forms such as ``Điều 5'', ``Điều 12a'', and ``Điều 32''. It also detects document terms such as ``Luật'', ``Bộ luật'', ``Nghị định'', and ``Thông tư''.

    \item \textbf{Filtering ambiguous phrases.} Keywords such as ``quy định'', ``quyết định'', and ``nghị quyết'' require a following number to be treated as document references. Ordinary expressions such as ``luật sư'' and ``luật chơi'' are excluded. The rules also filter references marked as drafts and remove trailing phrases, incomplete parentheses, and other text outside the detected document name. Document-specific exceptions handle cases not covered by the general rules.

    \item \textbf{Linking articles to documents.} The extractor first attempts to link an article mention to the following document mention when the intervening words are permitted connectors. If that association fails, it checks the preceding document using a separate connector-word set. This handles both ``Điều 9 Nghị định 79/2014/NĐ-CP'' and ``Nghị định số 79/2014/NĐ-CP tại Điều 9, Điều 15''. A document cited without a specific article is recorded with an empty article identifier, representing a whole-document target.

    \item \textbf{Removing duplicate references.} Repeated document and article pairs are removed. When two extracted names refer to the same article and one name is contained in a longer version, the more complete name is retained. For example, ``Luật Giao thông đường bộ năm 2008'' is retained over the shorter ``Luật Giao thông đường bộ'' when both have been extracted for the same article.
\end{enumerate}

The extracted references are saved with the cleaned question-answer record. Section~\ref{sec:bca-target-mapping} describes their mapping to corpus identifiers and the checks for target availability. Evaluation uses the matching rules in Section~\ref{sec:target-metrics}: a whole-document target can match any indexed article from that document, and each recorded target receives credit at most once.

\subsection{Target Mapping and Corpus Coverage}
\label{sec:bca-target-mapping}

Extracted legal references are mapped to corpus identifiers to construct evaluation targets. A correction table associates extracted document names with document identifiers. For example, ``Nghị định số 100/2019/NĐ-CP'' is mapped to \texttt{100/2019/nd-cp}. Leading and trailing whitespace is removed from document and article identifiers, and both are converted to lowercase. Each target consists of a document identifier and an article identifier. The article identifier is left empty when the published answer cites a document without specifying an article. These normalization steps standardize identifiers but do not verify that the extracted reference has been mapped to the correct legal document.

The reference corpus is extended with legal documents collected from public sources in raw HTML format. Article text is extracted from these documents and added to the corpus, while the raw HTML and source mappings are saved. Duplicate article identifiers within each document and articles with empty text are excluded. The resulting searchable corpus contains 14,789 articles.

References that remain unresolved may be assigned the placeholder \texttt{INTERNAL\_DOCUMENT}. Entries with this placeholder are excluded from corpus additions, but their questions and recorded targets remain in the evaluation set. The placeholder indicates an unresolved mapping, it does not establish that the referenced document is internal or access-restricted.

Target coverage is checked against the indexed article identifiers using the matching rules in Section~\ref{sec:target-metrics}. An article-level target requires a match on both the document and article identifiers. A whole-document target requires at least one indexed article with the same document identifier. A question has full coverage when all its recorded targets have a match, partial coverage when only some have a match, and no coverage when none has a match. These categories measure coverage of the recorded targets under the matching rules, rather than completeness of the corpus for answering the legal question.

A target without a match can result from missing corpus content or an unresolved or incorrect mapping. If the referenced article is absent from the corpus, no ranking method can retrieve it. If its text is present but the target is mapped incorrectly, retrieval may recover the relevant article without receiving evaluation credit. For a correctly mapped target present in the corpus, failure to retrieve a match within the evaluation cutoff concerns ranking performance. Therefore, coverage checks alone cannot establish whether a retrieval failure is caused by missing content, incorrect mapping, or ranking.

\section{Overview of Retrieval Experiments}
\label{sec:system-overview}

The retrieval experiments use Vietnamese questions as queries and legal article text as the searchable corpus. BM25 computes lexical scores, while BGE-M3 and multilingual-E5-base, referred to as E5, compute dense similarity scores. Each retriever scores every article in the same corpus order, ensuring that scores combined across retrievers refer to the same article. The experiments evaluate BM25, BGE-M3, and E5 individually, as well as the hybrid configurations BM25+BGE-M3 and BM25+BGE-M3+E5.

For hybrid retrieval, each retriever's scores are normalized separately for each query over the full corpus. The normalized scores are combined using a weighted sum, and articles are ranked by the combined score. Fusion weights are selected on the weight-selection queries and fixed before ranking the separate evaluation queries. The system returns the ten highest-ranked articles with their document and article identifiers. The returned identifiers are compared with the recorded relevance targets to compute NDCG@10 and the other evaluation metrics. Section~\ref{sec:score-fusion} specifies score normalization and fusion, and Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-design} describes query grouping and the allocation of queries to weight selection and evaluation. Figure~\ref{fig:4.1} summarizes this procedure with pretrained dense models.

The fine-tuning comparisons replace pretrained BGE-M3 with a fine-tuned model while keeping BM25, E5, and the corresponding fusion weights fixed. Standalone dense retrieval is evaluated separately to examine whether changes in BGE-M3's retrieval performance are also observed in the hybrid configurations. Section~\ref{sec:bge-finetuning} describes the training and model selection procedures. The evaluated system produces article rankings; reranking and answer generation are outside its scope.

\section{Retrieval Models and Implementation}
\label{sec:retrieval-implementation}

The experiments use BM25 for lexical retrieval and the dense representations of BGE-M3 and multilingual-E5-base for semantic retrieval. All models score the same ordered articles within each corpus version. Preserving article order allows their scores to be combined without changing the associated legal document and article identifiers.

\subsection{BM25}

BM25 scores tokenized question and article text. Preprocessing converts text to lowercase, replaces non-word punctuation with token separators, and splits the resulting text on whitespace. This preprocessing remains fixed in the reported comparisons. The experiments therefore evaluate this BM25 configuration rather than alternative Vietnamese tokenization methods.

\subsection{BGE-M3 and Multilingual-E5-base}

BGE-M3 is the primary dense retriever. Multilingual-E5-base, referred to as E5, provides an additional dense score for the BM25+BGE-M3+E5 configuration. The experiments use BGE-M3's dense representation; its sparse and multi-vector retrieval methods are not evaluated.

Each encoder maps the question and each article to vectors. The implementation divides each vector by its Euclidean norm, with a minimum denominator of \(10^{-10}\), and computes the dot product of the normalized vectors. For the query vector \(\mathbf{u}_q\) and article vector \(\mathbf{v}_d\) before normalization, the resulting score is
\[
s_{\mathrm{dense}}(q,d)=\frac{\mathbf{u}_q^\top\mathbf{v}_d}{\max(\lVert\mathbf{u}_q\rVert_2,10^{-10})\max(\lVert\mathbf{v}_d\rVert_2,10^{-10})}.
\]
Here, \(q\) is the question, \(d\) is an indexed article, and \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_2\) denotes the Euclidean norm. For vectors whose norms exceed the numerical threshold, this calculation is cosine similarity. The threshold prevents division by zero.

\section{Score Normalization and Fusion}
\label{sec:score-fusion}

BM25 and dense retrieval produce scores on different scales. Before combining them, the baseline applies Min-Max normalization separately to each retriever's scores for each query. For retriever \(r\), raw score \(s_r(q,d)\), and full indexed corpus \(C\), the normalized score is
\[
\widetilde{s}_r(q,d)=\frac{s_r(q,d)-\min_{d'\in C}s_r(q,d')}{\max\left(\max_{d'\in C}s_r(q,d')-\min_{d'\in C}s_r(q,d'),\epsilon\right)}.
\]
Here, \(\epsilon\) is a small positive constant that prevents division by zero. If a retriever assigns the same score to every article for a query, all its normalized scores are zero. Normalization uses the full corpus rather than a shortlist of retrieved candidates, so every indexed article can enter the final ranking.

The BM25+BGE-M3 score is
\[
S_{bg}(q,d)
=w_b\widetilde{s}_{b}(q,d)+(1-w_b)\widetilde{s}_{g}(q,d),
\]
where \(b\) denotes BM25, \(g\) denotes BGE-M3, and \(0\leq w_b\leq1\). Adding E5 gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{bge}(q,d)
&=w_b\widetilde{s}_{b}(q,d)+w_g\widetilde{s}_{g}(q,d)
+w_e\widetilde{s}_{e}(q,d),\\
w_b+w_g+w_e&=1,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(e\) denotes E5 and all weights are nonnegative. The articles are ranked by the combined score in decreasing order.

The baseline selects weights from a grid with increments of 0.1. BM25+BGE-M3 has 11 candidate settings, with \(w_b\in\{0,0.1,\ldots,1\}\). BM25+BGE-M3+E5 has 66 candidate settings whose weights sum to one. This grid includes zero-weight settings, including \(w_e=0\), so it can reproduce every BM25+BGE-M3 setting in the baseline grid. A higher score on the weight-selection queries therefore does not establish that E5 improves retrieval on separate evaluation queries.

For each baseline split, the weights with the highest mean NDCG@10 on the weight-selection queries are fixed before evaluation on the other queries. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-design} describes the grouping and fold procedure. Algorithm~\ref{alg:locked-hybrid-ranking} shows how the selected configuration produces a ranking for one query.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Article ranking with fixed fusion weights}
\label{alg:locked-hybrid-ranking}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Query $q$, corpus $C$, retriever scores, fixed fusion weights $w$
\Ensure Ten highest-ranked articles
\State Normalize each retriever's scores over the full corpus for $q$
\For{each article $d\in C$}
    \State $\mathrm{combined}[d]\gets\sum_r w_r\,\mathrm{normalized\_score}[r,d]$
\EndFor
\State \Return the ten articles with highest combined score
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The fine-tuning comparisons use weights selected with pretrained BGE-M3 and keep them fixed when replacing it with the fine-tuned model. BM25 and E5 also remain unchanged. These comparisons measure the effect of replacing BGE-M3 within the specified hybrid configuration. The 0.1 grid described above applies to baseline selection. The fine-tuning experiments use separate selection procedures, described in Section~\ref{sec:bge-finetuning}.

\section{BGE-M3 Fine-Tuning}
\label{sec:bge-finetuning}

The two fine-tuning experiments differ in how they select BGE-M3. The first selects the model using hybrid retrieval performance on development queries, followed by evaluation on a separate test set. The second selects it using standalone dense retrieval performance, then evaluates the selected model both on its own and in BM25+BGE-M3 and BM25+BGE-M3+E5. Within each hybrid comparison, fine-tuned BGE-M3 replaces pretrained BGE-M3 while BM25, E5, and the selected fusion weights remain fixed. These comparisons test whether fine-tuning improves standalone retrieval and whether any improvement is retained in the hybrids under fixed fusion weights. The experiments use different training procedures and are therefore described separately. Section~\ref{sec:finetuning-results} reports the results and evaluation limitations.

\subsection{Model Selection Based on Hybrid Retrieval}
\label{sec:finetuning-hybrid-selection}

This experiment fine-tunes BGE-M3 on the Zalo dataset and selects its training settings using hybrid retrieval performance on development queries.

\subsubsection{Training Data and Negative Examples}

For each eligible question, one article labeled as relevant is selected. The article must be available in both the reference-text and extracted-text corpus versions. The question is paired with a article to create two training groups. Each group contains one positive article and seven explicit negative articles.

Negative candidates are taken from up to 256 of the highest-ranked results of each of BM25 and pretrained BGE-M3 using the extracted-text corpus. Filtering removes known relevant articles, duplicate candidates, unavailable articles, and articles from legal documents reserved for development or test evaluation. Articles from the same legal document as the positive are also excluded to reduce the risk of selecting a related but unannotated article as a negative.




\subsubsection{Training Objective and Configuration}

The training objective is to make BGE-M3 assign a higher similarity score to the selected relevant article than to the other articles in the batch. Each question is supplied with one positive article and seven explicit negatives. Articles from other question groups in the same batch provide additional comparisons. Only BGE-M3's parameters are updated during this training.

A batch contains \(B\) question groups, with \(G=8\) articles per group. The normalized vectors for question \(i\) and article \(j\) are \(\mathbf q_i\) and \(\mathbf p_j\). The selected positive article is at position \(y_i=iG\), because it is stored first in each group. The training loss is
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{batch}}=-\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=0}^{B-1}
\log\frac{\exp(\mathbf q_i^\top\mathbf p_{y_i}/\tau)}
{\sum_{j=0}^{BG-1}\exp(\mathbf q_i^\top\mathbf p_j/\tau)}.
\]

The dot product measures similarity, and \(\tau=0.02\) scales these scores before the loss is calculated. The numerator uses the selected positive article, while the denominator includes all articles in the batch. The loss decreases as the positive article's score increases relative to the others. The final loss is averaged over the \(B\) questions.

With the selected batch size of four question groups, each question is compared with 32 article vectors. Gradients are accumulated over eight forward passes before updating the model, so one update uses 32 question groups. Each loss calculation still compares only the articles within its own forward pass.

\subsection{Model Selection Based on Standalone Dense Retrieval}
\label{sec:finetuning-dense-selection}

\subsubsection{Training Data and Configuration}

This experiment selects BGE-M3 using standalone dense retrieval before testing it in BM25+BGE-M3 and BM25+BGE-M3+E5. It uses a fixed corpus of 61,425 parsed articles, with reference text retained where parsed text is unavailable. The official training partition contains 2,556 queries. Removing one near-duplicate of an official test query leaves 2,555 eligible training groups. The final evaluation uses 640 official test queries.

Each group includes five explicit negatives. The training procedure uses a mask to identify all known positive articles within the batch candidate set. This differs from the selected-positive objective in Section~\ref{sec:finetuning-hybrid-selection}. The encoder uses the hidden state at the first token position, L2 normalization, and query and passage limits of 256 and 512 tokens, respectively. The specified temperature is 0.05, with bfloat16 precision, a cosine learning-rate schedule, and a warmup ratio of 0.05. The final runs use a contrastive batch of 64 queries with gradient caching and chunks of two sequences, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:colab-training-runtime}.

\subsubsection{Training Objective}

Utilising hard negatives, the training objective is to maximize the similarity score of each query with its known positive articles while minimizing the score with all other articles in the batch. The experiment loop thought configuration finetune to find the best model based on the highest NDCG@10 on the development set. The selected model is then evaluated on the official test queries, both as a standalone dense retriever and within the hybrid configurations.

\subsubsection{Model Selection and Hybrid Evaluation}

BGE-M3 is selected using standalone NDCG@10 on development data, with Recall@100 checked to limit losses in target recovery. This evaluates the embedding model's retrieval performance before it is combined with BM25 and E5.

The selected model is then evaluated in BM25+BGE-M3 and BM25+BGE-M3+E5 on the same test dataset used for the standalone comparison. Fusion weights are selected using pretrained BGE-M3 on training queries. These weights, BM25, and E5 remain fixed when fine-tuned BGE-M3 replaces the pretrained model. This comparison tests whether a standalone improvement is retained after score fusion. Section~\ref{sec:finetuning-results} reports the results and evaluation limitations.

\section{Evaluation Design and Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:evaluation-design}

\subsection{Query Grouping and Data Splits}

Questions that cite the same legal document are kept in one group. A question citing several documents links their groups together. This prevents questions linked by shared legal references from being split between weight selection and evaluation.

For the pretrained ALQAC and Zalo comparisons, one fold is evaluated using fusion weights selected on the remaining folds. Repeating this procedure gives each question one evaluation result without using its labels to select the weights applied to it. The query populations are defined in Section~\ref{sec:datasets-corpus}.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:grouped-fusion-selection} summarizes the procedure. Metrics are averaged over all evaluation queries, giving each query equal weight. The selected weights may differ between folds.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Grouped selection and evaluation of fusion weights}
\label{alg:grouped-fusion-selection}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Ordered queries $Q$, corpus $D$, aligned normalized score matrices, grouped folds, ordered candidate weight grid $W$
\Ensure Selected weights per fold and evaluation metrics for every query
\For{each fold $f$}
    \State Use the other folds for selection ($T$) and fold $f$ for evaluation ($V$)
    \For{each candidate weight vector $w\in W$, in recorded order}
        \State Rank $D$ for every query in $T$ using the weighted score sum
        \State Compute mean query NDCG@10
    \EndFor
    \State Select the first candidate with the highest selection mean
    \State Rank all queries in $V$ using the selected weights
    \State Store each evaluation query's metrics
\EndFor
\State Aggregate evaluation-query rows across folds, giving each query equal weight
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


The fine-tuning experiments use the training, development, and test allocations in Section~\ref{sec:bge-finetuning}. Each fine-tuned model is compared with its matched pretrained baseline on the same test dataset.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics and Comparisons}

NDCG@10 is the primary metric because it rewards matches at higher ranks. Hit@10 records whether any target is retrieved, MRR@10 measures how early the first match occurs, and MAP@10 summarizes precision at matching ranks. The experiment selected using standalone dense performance also reports Recall@100 for its standalone and hybrid comparisons. Section~\ref{sec:target-metrics} defines the matching rules and formulas.

The evaluation separates three comparisons:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hybrid retrieval versus BM25:} compare BM25+BGE-M3 with BM25 to assess the benefit of combining lexical and dense retrieval.
    \item \textbf{Contribution of E5:} compare BM25+BGE-M3+E5 with BM25+BGE-M3 to assess the additional contribution of E5.
    \item \textbf{Effect of fine-tuning:} compare pretrained and fine-tuned BGE-M3, both on its own and within the hybrids. Within each hybrid comparison, BM25, E5, and the combination weights remain fixed.
\end{itemize}
A practically useful hybrid improvement requires an absolute NDCG@10 increase of at least 0.01. Statistical uncertainty is assessed separately.



\section{Experimental Environment and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:experimental-environment}

The experiments run on Google Colab with a single NVIDIA L4 GPU. A local machine launches the runtime and monitors its logs through the Google Colab command-line interface. Figure~\ref{fig:colab-training-runtime} shows the execution environment for the fine-tuning experiment that selects BGE-M3 using standalone dense retrieval.

Code and data are copied from Google Drive to the runtime disk and monitor task progress. Training runs as a background Python process, with seeds 42, 43, and 44 executed sequentially for 60 optimizer steps each. Gradient caching supports a contrastive batch of 64 queries while processing chunks of two sequences. 

Checkpoints are saved at steps 30 and 60, verified, reloaded, and copied to Google Drive. The runtime disk holds temporary files, while Google Drive retains the files needed to resume from a valid checkpoint after a runtime interruption.

\section{Research Ethics and Data Use}
\label{sec:ethics-data}

The experiments use the ALQAC and Zalo collections and the BCA dataset described in Sections~\ref{sec:datasets-corpus} and~\ref{sec:bca-construction}. BCA is derived from public questions and published answers on the Bộ Công an portal. Public access identifies the source of these records, but does not by itself establish permission for unrestricted reuse or redistribution. The construction records described in this thesis do not establish consent from the people who submitted the questions or document a completed review of personal information.

Public questions and answers may contain details that identify individuals. Any future release of BCA therefore requires a review of identifying information, source terms, and the permitted scope of redistribution. The example in Section~\ref{sec:bca-construction} summarizes the query and target structure without reproducing personal details. Published answers are retained as evidence for reference extraction and are not supplied as query inputs during retrieval.

The evaluation measures recovery of recorded legal references. These references are extracted from published answers and may be incomplete or incorrectly mapped, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:bca-construction}. Retrieval scores therefore do not establish the correctness of a legal answer or the current applicability of a retrieved provision. The experiments assess article ranking and do not evaluate a system for providing legal advice.

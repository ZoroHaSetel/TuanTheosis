\chapter{Literature Review}

This chapter reviews prior work on legal retrieval, multilingual embeddings, and hybrid fusion. It identifies the research gap addressed by this thesis and positions its contributions relative to the literature.

Figure~\ref{fig:rag-system-position} shows the position of retrieval in a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system. The retrieval component searches the document sources and supplies information to the generator to support answer generation. If the retriever fails to return relevant information, the generator cannot produce a correct answer. Therefore, retrieval performance is a prerequisite for accurate answer generation.

\section{Legal Retrieval and Evidence}

Legal information retrieval identifies and ranks legal materials relevant to a query. The retrieval unit may be a judgment, statute, article, or passage. The Competition on Legal Information Extraction and Entailment (COLIEE) separates retrieval tasks from entailment tasks, which assess whether legal texts support a given statement. Its case tasks use Canadian case law, while its statute tasks use Japanese bar-examination questions~\cite{goebel2024}. Comparisons with Vietnamese article retrieval must therefore account for differences in the legal system, retrieval unit, relevance annotations, and evaluation metrics.

The Automated Legal Question Answering Competition (ALQAC) 2024 provides manually annotated Vietnamese legal questions and defines separate tasks for legal document retrieval and question answering. In the retrieval task, articles are relevant when they can be used to answer the question~\cite{alqac2024}. This thesis uses the available relevance annotations to assess how highly the recorded relevant articles are ranked.

Similarity in wording or topic alone does not establish relevance to a legal question. For example, a question about an application deadline may match an article concerning a different stage of the same procedure. In this hypothetical case, the article discusses the same topic but does not provide the required deadline. A relevant provision may also use different terms from those in the question. These possibilities motivate comparing lexical and dense retrieval using the same queries and relevance annotations.

The retrieval unit must also match the relevance annotations. An article may contain rules, exceptions, and references to other provisions, so splitting it into passages changes the context available to the retriever. When the annotations identify articles, retrieved passages must be mapped to their source articles before ranking performance is evaluated. Otherwise, passage retrieval and article retrieval would be assessed using different units.

Text preprocessing is another consideration in Vietnamese retrieval. PhoBERT uses word segmentation before subword tokenization and is evaluated on Vietnamese language-processing tasks~\cite{nguyen2020}. Its preprocessing illustrates a model-specific requirement, its results do not establish that word segmentation improves the retrievers used in this thesis. For lexical retrieval, splitting text at spaces and segmenting it into words produce different vocabularies and term counts. For dense retrieval, preprocessing should follow the embedding model's documented requirements.

In the RAG workflow shown in Figure~\ref{fig:rag-system-position}, retrieval supplies information to the generator. Lewis et al. combine a language model with retrieved passages for knowledge-intensive tasks~\cite{lewis2020}. This thesis evaluates the retrieval component by measuring article ranking against the available relevance annotations. Therefore, its results establish retrieval performance within those annotations, while any improvement in the accuracy of generated legal answers requires a separate evaluation.

\section{Lexical Retrieval and BM25}

Lexical retrieval ranks documents by matching query terms with indexed text. BM25 is a probabilistic ranking model that combines term frequency and document frequency with document length normalization~\cite{robertson2009}. Its scores therefore depend on both the terms in a document and their distribution in the indexed corpus.

A BM25 submission achieved second place in the legal case retrieval task of COLIEE 2021~\cite{rosa2021}. This finding provides empirical support for using BM25 as a baseline in legal retrieval. Since that task concerns case law, its results do not establish BM25's effectiveness for Vietnamese legal article retrieval. The latter requires evaluation on the Vietnamese collections studied here.

This thesis compares dense and hybrid retrieval with BM25 using the same queries, corpus, and relevance annotations within each evaluation condition. BM25's evaluation scores serve as the baseline. Section~3.2 explains the scoring formula, and Section~\ref{sec:retrieval-implementation} specifies the preprocessing and retrieval implementation used in the experiments.

\section{Dense Retrieval and Multilingual Embeddings}

Dense retrieval represents queries and documents as embeddings and ranks documents based on their vector similarity. One approach is Sentence-BERT, which generates sentence embeddings that can be compared using cosine similarity~\cite{reimers2019}. Building on the idea of embedding-based retrieval, Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) uses separate encoders for queries and passages, specifically designed for open-domain question answering~\cite{karpukhin2020}. By encoding queries and passages independently, DPR allows passage embeddings to be precomputed and reused across queries, improving retrieval efficiency.

Recent embedding models have further extended dense retrieval to multilingual settings. BGE-M3 supports multilingual retrieval through three approaches: dense, sparse, and multi-vector representations~\cite{chen2024}. Similarly, multilingual E5 provides text embeddings across multiple languages, with small, base, and large model variants trained on multilingual text pairs and labeled datasets~\cite{wang2024}. Based on these capabilities, this thesis focuses on the dense representations of BGE-M3 and the multilingual-E5-base (E5) model. The evaluation is limited to these configurations, and the findings do not necessarily extend to other retrieval modes or model variants.

While BGE-M3 and E5 can represent text using a single dense vector, other approaches capture more detailed interactions between queries and documents. For example, ColBERT retains contextualized token embeddings and calculates relevance through token-level interactions rather than relying on a single vector for each text~\cite{khattab2020}. Although ColBERT offers an alternative retrieval approach, it is discussed only as related work and is not included in the experiments.

This thesis focuses on BGE-M3 and multilingual-E5-base (E5) to investigate whether combining different dense retrieval models can improve retrieval performance. Since the two models differ in their training procedures and embedding methods~\cite{chen2024,wang2024}, they may produce complementary rankings. In particular, E5 may help retrieve relevant legal articles that BGE-M3 ranks poorly. To examine this possibility, the experiments compare BM25+BGE-M3+E5 against BM25+BGE-M3.

In addition, BM25, pretrained BGE-M3, and E5 are evaluated individually to establish their retrieval performance on Vietnamese legal datasets. The combination of BM25+BGE-M3 is then compared with BM25 to measure the benefit of combining lexical and semantic retrieval. Together, these comparisons help distinguish the contribution of hybrid retrieval from the additional benefit of incorporating E5.

\section{Hybrid Retrieval and Score Fusion}
Hybrid retrieval combines results from multiple retrievers to improve document ranking. Since lexical and dense retrieval use different methods to identify relevant documents, combining their results may help retrieve articles that one method alone ranks poorly. One common approach is Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF), which combines rankings based on document positions rather than retrieval scores~\cite{cormack2009}. Another approach is weighted score fusion, which combines retrieval scores using assigned weights. Bruch et al. investigate how score normalization and weight selection affect the combination of lexical and semantic retrieval~\cite{bruch2022}. Their findings highlight the importance of defining how scores are normalized and how fusion weights are selected.

The effectiveness of hybrid retrieval has also been studied in Vietnamese retrieval tasks. Nguyen and Quan evaluate lexical, dense, hybrid, and other retrieval methods across ten Vietnamese datasets covering six domains, including law~\cite{nguyenquan2026}. Their work provides a broader understanding of retrieval performance in Vietnamese, while this thesis focuses specifically on retrieving Vietnamese legal articles.

Building on these studies, this thesis evaluates hybrid retrieval through several comparisons. First, BM25+BGE-M3 is compared with BM25 to determine whether combining lexical and dense retrieval improves performance. Next, BM25+BGE-M3+E5 is compared with BM25+BGE-M3 to examine whether adding a second dense retriever provides further improvement. This distinction is important because strong standalone performance from E5 does not necessarily mean it will improve an existing hybrid.

The thesis also investigates whether fine-tuning BGE-M3 improves retrieval performance. To assess this, pretrained and fine-tuned BGE-M3 are compared both individually and within the hybrid systems. BM25, E5, and fusion weights remain unchanged during these comparisons, allowing the effect of replacing pretrained BGE-M3 with its fine-tuned version to be examined. This also helps determine whether improvements in standalone dense retrieval translate into better hybrid performance. Section~\ref{sec:weighted-score-fusion} explains weighted score fusion, and Section~\ref{sec:score-fusion} specifies its implementation. Reciprocal Rank Fusion is included as related work and is not evaluated in the experiments.

\section{Fine-Tuning for Legal Retrieval and Hard Negatives}

Beyond combining different retrievers, retrieval performance can also be improved by fine-tuning dense models. A common approach is contrastive training, which teaches a model to assign higher similarity scores to relevant passages than to irrelevant ones~\cite{karpukhin2020}. An important part of this process is selecting negative examples that help the model learn meaningful distinctions.

One approach is to use hard negatives, which are irrelevant passages that the retriever ranks highly because they appear similar to the query. Training with these examples helps the model distinguish relevant passages from closely related but incorrect ones. Zhan et al. show that combining hard negatives with random negatives or updating hard negatives during training can improve dense retrieval performance~\cite{zhan2021}.

Negative selection has also been explored in Vietnamese legal retrieval. Le et al. propose a two-stage approach that combines a fine-tuned bi-encoder with a cross-encoder reranker and uses negative mining during training~\cite{le2025semihard}. Their experiments in the SoICT Hackathon 2024 setting show that semi-hard negatives can improve reranking performance. However, their results focus on a two-stage retrieval system and do not establish whether fine-tuning a dense retriever improves its contribution to hybrid score fusion.

Building on these findings, this thesis investigates whether fine-tuning BGE-M3 can improve Vietnamese legal article retrieval. Unlike the two-stage approach, the experiments focus on standalone dense retrieval and weighted hybrid retrieval without reranking. This allows the study to examine whether improvements from fine-tuning BGE-M3 also lead to better rankings when its scores are combined with those of other retrievers.

\section{Research Gap and Positioning}
Previous studies have explored several approaches to improving retrieval performance, including multilingual embedding models, hybrid retrieval, and dense retriever fine-tuning. BGE-M3 and multilingual E5 provide pretrained models for multilingual semantic retrieval~\cite{chen2024,wang2024}. Bruch et al. examine how score normalization and weight selection affect hybrid retrieval~\cite{bruch2022}, while Zhan et al. demonstrate the benefits of hard-negative training for dense retrieval~\cite{zhan2021}. Together, these studies provide a foundation for investigating how these approaches perform on Vietnamese legal documents.

Related research has also examined retrieval across Vietnamese domains, hybrid retrieval with in-domain training for French legal texts, and negative mining for Vietnamese legal retrieval~\cite{nguyenquan2026,louis2025,le2025semihard}. Building on this work, this thesis investigates two main questions. First, whether adding multilingual E5 improves retrieval beyond the BM25+BGE-M3 hybrid. Second, whether fine-tuning BGE-M3 improves its standalone performance and its contribution to hybrid retrieval when fusion weights remain unchanged. These questions are evaluated using selected Vietnamese legal datasets, including the newly constructed BCA collection.

The main contribution of this thesis is a systematic empirical evaluation of established retrieval methods under controlled experimental conditions, together with the construction and analysis of the BCA collection. The BCA analysis examines the availability of cited legal provisions and the relationships between questions that share legal references, which are important considerations for reliable evaluation. Chapter~4 describes the datasets and experimental procedures, while Chapter~5 presents and discusses the results.

\section{Chapter Summary}

This chapter reviewed legal retrieval, BM25, multilingual embeddings, score fusion, and fine-tuning with hard negatives. The reviewed studies motivate evaluating these methods on Vietnamese legal collections, with separate comparisons for improvements over BM25, the contribution of E5, and the effect of BGE-M3 fine-tuning in standalone and hybrid retrieval. Chapter~3 presents the theoretical background and evaluation metrics used in these comparisons.

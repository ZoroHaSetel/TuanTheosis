\chapter{Literature Review}

This chapter reviews prior work on legal retrieval, multilingual embeddings, and hybrid fusion. It identifies the research gap addressed by this thesis and positions its contributions relative to the literature.

Figure~\ref{fig:rag-system-position} shows the position of retrieval in a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system. The retrieval component searches the document sources and supplies information to the generator to support answer generation. If the retriever fails to return relevant information, the generator cannot produce a correct answer. Therefore, retrieval performance is a prerequisite for accurate answer generation.

\section{Legal Retrieval and Evidence}

Legal information retrieval identifies and ranks legal materials relevant to a query. The retrieval unit may be a judgment, statute, article, or passage. The Competition on Legal Information Extraction and Entailment (COLIEE) separates retrieval tasks from entailment tasks, which assess whether legal texts support a given statement. Its case tasks use Canadian case law, while its statute tasks use Japanese bar-examination questions~\cite{goebel2024}. Comparisons with Vietnamese article retrieval must therefore account for differences in the legal system, retrieval unit, relevance annotations, and evaluation metrics.

The Automated Legal Question Answering Competition (ALQAC) 2024 provides manually annotated Vietnamese legal questions and defines separate tasks for legal document retrieval and question answering. In the retrieval task, articles are relevant when they can be used to answer the question~\cite{alqac2024}. This thesis uses the available relevance annotations to assess how highly the recorded relevant articles are ranked.

Similarity in wording or topic alone does not establish relevance to a legal question. For example, a question about an application deadline may match an article concerning a different stage of the same procedure. In this hypothetical case, the article discusses the same topic but does not provide the required deadline. A relevant provision may also use different terms from those in the question. These possibilities motivate comparing lexical and dense retrieval using the same queries and relevance annotations.

The retrieval unit must also match the relevance annotations. An article may contain rules, exceptions, and references to other provisions, so splitting it into passages changes the context available to the retriever. When the annotations identify articles, retrieved passages must be mapped to their source articles before ranking performance is evaluated. Otherwise, passage retrieval and article retrieval would be assessed using different units.

Text preprocessing is another consideration in Vietnamese retrieval. PhoBERT uses word segmentation before subword tokenization and is evaluated on Vietnamese language-processing tasks~\cite{nguyen2020}. Its preprocessing illustrates a model-specific requirement; its results do not establish that word segmentation improves the retrievers used in this thesis. For lexical retrieval, splitting text at spaces and segmenting it into words produce different vocabularies and term counts. For dense retrieval, preprocessing should follow the embedding model's documented requirements.

In the RAG workflow shown in Figure~\ref{fig:rag-system-position}, retrieval supplies information to the generator. Lewis et al. combine a language model with retrieved passages for knowledge-intensive tasks~\cite{lewis2020}. This thesis evaluates the retrieval component by measuring article ranking against the available relevance annotations. Therefore, its results establish retrieval performance within those annotations, while any improvement in the accuracy of generated legal answers requires a separate evaluation.

\section{Lexical Retrieval and BM25}

Lexical retrieval ranks documents by matching query terms with indexed text. BM25 is a probabilistic ranking model that combines term frequency and document frequency with document length normalization~\cite{robertson2009}. Its scores therefore depend on both the terms in a document and their distribution in the indexed corpus.

A BM25 submission achieved second place in the legal case retrieval task of COLIEE 2021~\cite{rosa2021}. This finding provides empirical support for using BM25 as a baseline in legal retrieval. Since that task concerns case law, its results do not establish BM25's effectiveness for Vietnamese legal article retrieval. The latter requires evaluation on the Vietnamese collections studied here.

This thesis compares dense and hybrid retrieval with BM25 using the same queries, corpus, and relevance annotations within each evaluation condition. BM25's evaluation scores serve as the baseline. Section~3.2 explains the scoring formula, and Section~\ref{sec:retrieval-implementation} specifies the preprocessing and retrieval implementation used in the experiments.

\section{Dense Retrieval and Multilingual Embeddings}

Dense retrieval represents queries and documents as embeddings and ranks documents by vector similarity. Sentence-BERT produces sentence embeddings that can be compared using cosine similarity~\cite{reimers2019}. Dense Passage Retrieval trains separate query and passage encoders for open-domain question answering~\cite{karpukhin2020}. Independent encoding allows document embeddings to be computed before a query is submitted and reused during retrieval.

BGE-M3 supports multilingual retrieval through dense, sparse, and multiple-vector representations~\cite{chen2024}. Multilingual E5 provides embedding models in small, base, and large variants, trained using multilingual text pairs and labeled datasets~\cite{wang2024}. This thesis evaluates BGE-M3's dense representations and multilingual-E5-base (E5). The results therefore apply to these configurations, rather than to every retrieval mode or model variant described in the source papers.

Single-vector retrieval represents each query and document with one vector. ColBERT instead retains contextualized token embeddings and computes relevance through interactions between query and document tokens after encoding~\cite{khattab2020}. This approach provides an alternative to single-vector retrieval. ColBERT is included here as related work and is not evaluated in the experiments.

Multilingual-E5-base is included to test whether it complements BGE-M3 in semantic retrieval. The models use different training procedures and embedding methods~\cite{chen2024,wang2024}. The hypothesis is that these differences may produce complementary rankings, allowing E5 to improve retrieval for some questions whose relevant articles are ranked poorly by BGE-M3. Whether this improves hybrid retrieval is tested by comparing BM25+BGE-M3+E5 with BM25+BGE-M3.

The experiments also compare pretrained BGE-M3 and E5 with BM25 to assess standalone retrieval on the Vietnamese legal collections, and BM25+BGE-M3 with BM25 to assess hybrid retrieval. These comparisons distinguish the effect of combining lexical and dense retrieval from the effect of adding E5 to an existing hybrid.

\section{Hybrid Retrieval and Score Fusion}

Hybrid retrieval combines the outputs of different retrievers to produce a single ranking. Reciprocal Rank Fusion combines rankings using document positions rather than the original retrieval scores~\cite{cormack2009}. Weighted score fusion instead combines the component scores according to assigned weights. An analysis of lexical and semantic retrieval examines how score normalization and parameter selection affect fusion performance~\cite{bruch2022}. These findings motivate specifying both the normalization method and the procedure for selecting fusion weights.

The hypothesis is that lexical and dense retrieval may provide complementary rankings. A relevant article ranked poorly by one retriever may be ranked highly by another. Adding E5 to BM25+BGE-M3 tests whether another dense retriever improves a system that already combines lexical and semantic information. Its standalone performance does not establish that additional benefit.

These studies motivate evaluating whether combining lexical and dense retrieval improves Vietnamese legal article ranking, and whether an additional dense retriever provides further benefit. Section~\ref{sec:weighted-score-fusion} explains the fusion calculation, while Section~\ref{sec:score-fusion} specifies the experimental procedure. Reciprocal Rank Fusion is discussed as related work but is not evaluated in the experiments.

\section{Fine-Tuning for Legal Retrieval and Hard Negatives}

Contrastive training teaches a retrieval model to give relevant passages higher similarity scores than passages labeled as irrelevant~\cite{karpukhin2020}.

Hard negatives are passages that a retriever ranks highly even though they are labeled as irrelevant. They help train the model to distinguish relevant passages from similar but incorrect matches. Experiments show that mixing hard negatives with randomly selected negatives, or selecting new hard negatives during training, can improve dense retrieval~\cite{zhan2021}.

These findings motivate fine-tuning a dense retriever, and the experiments test whether fine-tuning BGE-M3 can improve retrieval on the Vietnamese legal collections. 
\section{Research Gap and Positioning}

BGE-M3 and multilingual E5 provide pretrained embedding models for multilingual retrieval~\cite{chen2024,wang2024}. Research on lexical and semantic score fusion examines the effects of normalization and parameter selection~\cite{bruch2022}, while hard-negative training strategies have improved dense retrieval on benchmark datasets~\cite{zhan2021}. These studies provide a basis for evaluating multilingual embeddings, score fusion, and embedding model fine-tuning on Vietnamese legal collections.

The research gap addressed by this thesis is the evidence needed to assess these methods under the selected Vietnamese legal datasets. The evaluation compares BM25, BGE-M3, and E5 in standalone and hybrid retrieval, and tests whether fine-tuning BGE-M3 with hard negatives improves retrieval.

The contribution is a controlled empirical comparison of established retrieval methods, together with the construction and evaluation of the BCA collection. The BCA analysis examines the availability of cited provisions and dependence among questions linked by shared legal references. Chapter~4 specifies the datasets and evaluation procedures, and Chapter~5 reports the results within those conditions.

\section{Chapter Summary}

This chapter reviewed legal retrieval, BM25, multilingual embeddings, score fusion, and fine-tuning with hard negatives. The reviewed studies motivate evaluating these methods on Vietnamese legal collections, with separate comparisons for improvements over BM25, the contribution of E5, and the effect of BGE-M3 fine-tuning in standalone and hybrid retrieval. Chapter~3 presents the theoretical background and evaluation metrics used in these comparisons.

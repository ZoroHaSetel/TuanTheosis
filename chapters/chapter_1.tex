\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Research Context}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is an AI framework that improves Large Language Models (LLMs) by connecting them to external knowledge bases before they generate an answer~\cite{lewis2020}. In legal question answering, retrieval precision is critical: RAG can reduces hallucinations, accesses private data, and anwser only base on the retrieved information. Accordingly, this thesis centers on retrieval effectiveness. Given a Vietnamese legal query and a corpus of legal documents, the goal is to rank provisions such that relevant articles appear at the top of the retrieved results.

Retrieving Vietnamese legal documents poses several distinct challenges. User queries typically rely on everyday language, whereas statutory provisions are framed in formal legal terminology. Furthermore, articles addressing similar domains often dictate divergent conditions, obligations, or procedural requirements. thus, topical or lexical similarity alone does not establish legal relevance. Collection completeness also sets an upper bound on performance, as unindexed provisions cannot be retrieved. Additionally, misalignments between reference annotations and document identifiers can distort benchmark metrics. 

To establish a baseline, this thesis employs BM25, which ranks documents via lexical matching a vital capability given that legal queries frequently contain explicit statutory identifiers, document numbers, or standard phrasing~\cite{robertson2009,rosa2021}. In contrast, dense retrieval leverages embedding similarity to match queries and documents across lexical gaps. We evaluate two representative pretrained models: BGE-M3 and multilingual-E5-base (E5)~\cite{chen2024,wang2024}, along with hybrid configurations (BM25+BGE-M3 and BM25+BGE-M3+E5) that combine lexical and dense retrieval scores.

We evaluate these approaches on benchmark Vietnamese legal datasets using established relevance annotations. Specifically, we investigate whether BM25+BGE-M3 improves upon the BM25 baseline, whether integrating E5 yields further gains, and whether fine-tuning BGE-M3 enhances standalone retrieval and sustains improvements within hybrid pipelines. Through these empirical evaluations, this thesis clarifies the effectiveness of hybrid retrieval strategies for Vietnamese legal information retrieval.

\section{Research Gap and Approach}

Prior research provides a basis for the methods evaluated in this thesis. Rosa et al. report that BM25 is a competitive baseline for legal case retrieval~\cite{rosa2021}. Chen et al. and Wang et al. present BGE-M3 and multilingual E5 and evaluate their embedding models on multilingual benchmarks~\cite{chen2024,wang2024}. Bruch et al. analyze fusion of lexical and semantic retrieval scores, including the effects of normalization and parameter selection~\cite{bruch2022}. These findings motivate comparisons between lexical retrieval, dense retrieval, and their combinations.

The scope of that evidence also matters. Legal case retrieval differs from retrieving statutory articles for Vietnamese questions. In heterogeneous retrieval evaluation, BEIR identifies limitations in the generalization of dense retrievers across datasets~\cite{thakur2021}. Zhan et al. show that training with hard negatives can improve dense retrieval on their evaluated benchmarks~\cite{zhan2021}. their findings concern dense-retriever training rather than the effect of fine-tuning BGE-M3 within the hybrids evaluated here. Therefore, the literature provides a rationale for testing these methods, while their effectiveness on the selected Vietnamese legal collections remains an empirical question.

The research gap addressed here is the evidence needed for two specific decisions: whether adding E5 improves BM25+BGE-M3, and whether fine-tuning BGE-M3 improves both standalone and hybrid retrieval. The thesis tests these questions under defined corpus conditions and separates model selection from evaluation on a separate test set. This is an empirical contribution rather than a new retrieval algorithm. Chapter~2 reviews the supporting literature, and Chapter~4 specifies the comparisons and evaluation procedures.

The approach has three parts. First, the overall retrieval benchmark evaluates ALQAC and Zalo using only the reference article text supplied with each dataset. All retrieval methods within a dataset use the same questions, corpus, and recorded relevance targets.

Second, a Bộ Công an (BCA) dataset is constructed from published public-service questions and legal citations in their answers. The evaluation examines whether the cited provisions are present in the corpus and identifies retrieval failures. The evaluation result is reported separately due to differences in data source from ALQAC and Zalo.

Third, the BGE-M3 embedding model is fine-tuned using legal question article pairs and evaluated in standalone and hybrid retrieval. BM25 and E5 remain unchanged, and fusion weights are held fixed when comparing pretrained and fine-tuned BGE-M3 within each hybrid. The analysis distinguishes development scores from results on test set queries.

\section{Research Objectives}

The main objective is to evaluate hybrid retrieval and fine-tuning of the BGE-M3 embedding model for Vietnamese legal information retrieval. The thesis also constructs a new dataset BCA for evaluation, the goal of which is to provide a more comprehensive assessment of retrieval performance in the Vietnamese legal domain. The thesis has four specific objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Compare BM25, pretrained BGE-M3, pretrained E5, and BM25+BGE-M3 on ALQAC and Zalo datasets. BM25's evaluation scores serve as the baseline.
    \item Determine whether adding E5 improves retrieval by comparing BM25+BGE-M3+E5 with BM25+BGE-M3.
    \item Evaluate whether fine-tuning BGE-M3 improves the performance in BM25+BGE-M3 and BM25+BGE-M3+E5.
    \item Construct and evaluate the BCA collection from published public-service questions and legal citations in their answers. Benchmark retrieval performance, examine the differences between public questions with Zalo and ALQAC.
\end{enumerate}

Retrieval effectiveness is measured primarily by NDCG@10. Hit@10, MRR@10, and MAP@10 provide additional measures of retrieval performance within the top ten results. Comparisons are reported separately for each dataset and corpus condition to establish the scope of any observed improvement.

\section{Research Contributions}

The thesis contribution is an empirical comparison of retrieval methods performance including BM25, BGE-M3, E5, BM25+BGE-M3, and BM25+BGE-M3+E5 on ALQAC, Zalo, and BCA. The evaluation measures retrieval performance on datasets ALQAC, Zalo, and BCA. The results show that hybrid retrieval improves NDCG@10 over BM25 in most evaluated conditions, while adding E5 provides little to no additional benefit (Tables~\ref{tab:5.3} and~\ref{tab:5.4}).

The second contribution is an evaluation of fine-tuning the BGE-M3 embedding model for standalone and hybrid retrieval. The first experiment selects the fine-tuned model based on hybrid retrieval performance on development data. The second experiment selects the model based on standalone dense retrieval performance on development data. The result shows fine-tuning improves metric NDCG@10 but does not produce a meaningful improvement in either hybrid when fusion weights are the same (Tables~\ref{tab:5.12} and~\ref{tab:5.13}).

The third contribution is the construction and evaluation of the BCA retrieval collection from published public-service questions and legal citations in their answers. The analysis examines the availability of cited provisions in the corpus and dependence among questions linked by shared legal references. The analysis also examines the sortfall failures of the BCA collection (Table~\ref{tab:5.5}).

\section{Scope of the Thesis}

The thesis evaluates the ranking of legal articles for Vietnamese questions on ALQAC, Zalo, and BCA. It compares BM25, BGE-M3, E5, and their hybrid configurations, and examines the effect of fine-tuning BGE-M3. The evaluation covers retrieval performance, corpus coverage, and the mapping of cited provisions to indexed documents. Answer generation, the quality of legal advice, user satisfaction, the currency of legal documents, and deployment performance are outside its scope.

The evaluation uses Vietnamese questions and legal texts. BGE-M3 and E5 are multilingual models, but the experiments do not assess retrieval in other languages or legal systems. Conclusions are limited to the evaluated datasets, corpus conditions, model configurations, and fusion weights. Applying the findings to other collections or downstream RAG systems requires further evaluation.

For BCA dataset the data is collected from publicly available questions and answers on the Ministry of Public Security's website on the date of collection. Many questions are refrencing private legal documents, which is inaccessible to the public. The evaluation does not attempt to access or retrieve any private legal documents, and the analysis is limited to publicly available information.
\section{Thesis Organization}

\textbf{Chapter~1: Introduction} presents Vietnamese legal information retrieval and the research gap addressed by the thesis. It states the research objectives, contributions, and scope.

\textbf{Chapter~2: Literature Review} reviews prior work on legal retrieval, BM25, multilingual embedding models, hybrid retrieval, and fine-tuning. It also discusses retrieval benchmarks and evaluation methods to establish the basis for the thesis's experimental comparisons.

\textbf{Chapter~3: Theoretical Background} explains lexical and dense retrieval, score normalization and fusion, and contrastive training. It defines relevance, evaluation metrics, and the principles used to account for dependence among queries when interpreting results.

\textbf{Chapter~4: Research Methodology} describes dataset and corpus construction, including the BCA collection, and the retrieval pipeline. It specifies fusion weight selection, BGE-M3 fine-tuning, and the procedures for model selection and evaluation.

\textbf{Chapter~5: Experiments and Evaluation} describes the experimental conditions and benchmark evaluation procedure. Its results and analysis cover the BCA collection, BGE-M3 fine-tuning, and the overall comparison of individual and hybrid retrieval methods, including the contribution of E5.

\textbf{Chapter~6: Conclusion and Future Work} summarizes the findings and contributions in relation to the research objectives. It discusses the limitations of the evaluation and outlines directions for future work.

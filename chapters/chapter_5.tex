\chapter{Experiments and Evaluation}

This chapter describes the experimental conditions and benchmark evaluation procedure, then presents the results and analysis. The results cover the Bộ Công an (BCA) collection, BGE-M3 fine-tuning relative to matched pretrained baselines, and the overall retrieval benchmark, including the contributions of hybrid retrieval and multilingual-E5-base (E5).

\section{Experimental Conditions}
\label{sec:results-experimental-conditions}

The benchmark uses the dataset ALQAC and Zalo, while BCA uses its separately constructed corpus. The retrieval models are BM25, \texttt{BAAI/bge-m3}, and \texttt{intfloat/multilingual-e5-base}. Baseline dense inputs are limited to 512 tokens. Dataset sizes, preprocessing, and model settings are specified in Sections~\ref{sec:datasets-corpus} and~\ref{sec:retrieval-implementation}.


The experiment were ran on a single NVIDIA L4 GPU on Google Colab platform. The reason is that the L4 GPU is a powerful and cost-effective option for training and fine-tuning machine learning models. It provides sufficient computational resources to handle the training of large language models like BGE-M3 and E5, while also being more accessible and affordable compared to higher-end GPUs. Additionally, Google Colab offers a convenient environment for running experiments without the need for extensive local hardware setup, making it an ideal choice for this research. 

\section{Benchmark \& Evaluation Procedure}
\label{sec:benchmark-evaluation-procedure}

The overall benchmark uses the article text supplied with the ALQAC and Zalo datasets. BCA is evaluated using the corpus constructed from collected legal documents. The fine-tuning experiments are evaluated separately, each comparing pretrained and fine-tuned BGE-M3 on the same corpus and test dataset. Sections~\ref{sec:datasets-corpus} and~\ref{sec:bca-construction} describe the datasets and corpus preparation.

The evaluation follows six steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Prepare the inputs.} Align question text, article text, and relevance targets by their document and article identifiers. All retrievers use the same article order within each comparison. Only the question text is supplied as the query.
    \item \textbf{Define the evaluation splits.} For the pretrained benchmark, keep questions linked by shared legal references in the same group. For ALQAC and Zalo, evaluate each fold using fusion weights selected on the remaining folds. Fine-tuning uses the training, development, and test allocations in Section~\ref{sec:bge-finetuning}.
    \item \textbf{Score and rank the articles.} BM25, BGE-M3, and E5 score every article in the corpus. Each model ranks articles by its own scores. For hybrid retrieval, apply Min-Max normalization separately to each model's scores for each query, then rank articles by their weighted sum (Section~\ref{sec:score-fusion}).
    \item \textbf{Select weights and fine-tuned models.} Select fusion weights using mean normalized discounted cumulative gain at rank ten (NDCG@10) on the selection queries. Select fine-tuned BGE-M3 using development performance in either hybrid or standalone dense retrieval, according to the experiment. For the fine-tuning comparisons, select fusion weights with pretrained BGE-M3 and keep them fixed when evaluating the fine-tuned model.
    \item \textbf{Calculate evaluation metrics.} Match ranked articles to the recorded targets using the rules in Section~\ref{sec:target-metrics}. NDCG@10 is the primary metric. Hit@10 records whether a target appears in the first ten results. Mean reciprocal rank at ten (MRR@10) measures how early the first match occurs, while mean average precision at ten (MAP@10) accounts for precision at each matching rank. The experiment selected using standalone dense retrieval also reports Recall@100 for its standalone and hybrid comparisons.
    \item \textbf{Compare retrieval performance.} Compare BM25+BGE-M3 with BM25 to assess improvement over the baseline, and BM25+BGE-M3+E5 with BM25+BGE-M3 to assess the contribution of E5. Evaluate fine-tuning by comparing pretrained and fine-tuned BGE-M3 on the same test dataset. Assess standalone and hybrid results separately, keeping BM25, E5, and fusion weights fixed within each hybrid comparison.
\end{enumerate}

The ALQAC and Zalo benchmark averages the evaluation scores of all queries, giving each query equal weight. Each query is evaluated once, using weights selected on other folds. The BCA aggregate describes performance over the 546 retained queries.


Scores are reported to four decimal places, with scientific notation for small nonzero values. Differences are calculated before rounding. Each fine-tuning experiment uses its own pretrained baseline, and results from the two selection procedures are reported separately.

The collection records two distinct obstacles to constructing retrieval targets. Reference extraction yields no target for the 106 excluded records. These records is still a law question but the answers did not contain any legal references. This is a common problem in public-service questions, where the answer may describe a legal requirement without citing the relevant decree or article. The hybrid-selected configuration increases development scores but lowers NDCG@10 for both hybrids in every reported seed. These results show that fine-tuning BGE-M3 can be effective for standalone dense retrieval, currently not improving hybrid retrieval performance.

Table~\ref{tab:5.4} shows a small NDCG@10 decrease on ALQAC and a small increase on Zalo. The observed contribution of E5 therefore differs between the two datasets.

\subsubsection{Highest-Scoring Retrieval Configurations}

On ALQAC, BM25+BGE-M3 has the highest observed NDCG@10, MRR@10, MAP@10, and Hit@10. Adding E5 slightly reduces all four metrics.

On Zalo, BM25+BGE-M3+E5 has the highest NDCG@10, MRR@10, and MAP@10, while BM25+BGE-M3 has the highest Hit@10.

For the full 546-query BCA collection, BM25+BGE-M3+E5 has the highest observed NDCG@10 and Hit@10, whereas BM25+BGE-M3 has the highest MRR@10 and MAP@10. These descriptive results do not establish an advantage across independent legal groups.

Across the evaluated conditions, both hybrids have higher NDCG@10 than BM25. The contribution of E5 varies by condition and metric. Overall, the highest-scoring configuration depends on the metric and dataset. These results do not establish a general E5 improvement.

\subsection{Summary of Results}



From the overall benchmark, pretrained BGE-M3 has higher NDCG@10 than BM25 and E5 on both ALQAC and Zalo.

Both hybrids BM25+BGE-M3 and BM25+BGE-M3+E5 have higher NDCG@10 than BM25 and the individual dense retrievers. The contribution of E5 is small and varies by condition.

The finetune version of BGE-M3 does not improve hybrid retrieval in both dataset experiments.
This provides evidence that the pretrained BGE-M3 is already well-suited for hybrid retrieval in the legal domain, and further fine-tuning does not yield significant improvements.

\chapter{Experiments and Evaluation}

This chapter describes the experimental conditions and benchmark evaluation procedure, then presents the results and analysis. The results cover the Bộ Công an (BCA) collection, BGE-M3 fine-tuning relative to matched pretrained baselines, and the overall retrieval benchmark, including the contributions of hybrid retrieval and multilingual-E5-base (E5).

\section{Experimental Conditions}
\label{sec:results-experimental-conditions}

The benchmark uses the dataset ALQAC and Zalo, while BCA uses its separately constructed corpus. The retrieval models are BM25, \texttt{BAAI/bge-m3}, and \texttt{intfloat/multilingual-e5-base}. Baseline dense inputs are limited to 512 tokens. Dataset sizes, preprocessing, and model settings are specified in Sections~\ref{sec:datasets-corpus} and~\ref{sec:retrieval-implementation}.


The experiment were ran on a single NVIDIA L4 GPU on Google Colab platform. The reason is that the L4 GPU is a powerful and cost-effective option for training and fine-tuning machine learning models. It provides sufficient computational resources to handle the training of large language models like BGE-M3 and E5, while also being more accessible and affordable compared to higher-end GPUs. Additionally, Google Colab offers a convenient environment for running experiments without the need for extensive local hardware setup, making it an ideal choice for this research. 

\section{Benchmark \& Evaluation Procedure}
\label{sec:benchmark-evaluation-procedure}

The benchmark is conducted on the ALQAC and Zalo datasets. BCA is evaluated using the corpus constructed from collected legal documents. The fine-tuning experiments are evaluated separately, each comparing pretrained and fine-tuned BGE-M3 on the same corpus and test dataset. Sections~\ref{sec:datasets-corpus} and~\ref{sec:bca-construction} describe the datasets and corpus preparation.

The evaluation follows six steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Prepare inputs: Align question text, article text, and relevance targets by their document and article identifiers. All retrievers use the same article order within each comparison. Only the question text is supplied as the query.
    \item Define the evaluation splits: Questions sharing legal references are grouped together to prevent leakage between weight selection and evaluation.
    \item Score and rank the articles: Apply BM25, BGE-M3, and E5 score all articles given a query (Section~\ref{sec:score-fusion}).
    \item Select weights and fine-tuned models: Select fusion weights by mean NDCG@10 and fine-tuned BGE-M3 by development performance in the experiment. Keep pretrained fusion weights fixed when evaluating fine-tuned BGE-M3.
    \item Calculate evaluation metrics: Match ranked articles to recorded targets using Section~\ref{sec:target-metrics}. NDCG@10 is the primary metric; Hit@10 indicates any match, MRR@10 captures the first match’s rank, and MAP@10 summarizes precision across matching ranks. The experiment using standalone dense model selection also reports Recall@100 for standalone and hybrid retrieval.
    \item Compare retrieval performance: Compare BM25+BGE-M3 with BM25 to assess improvement over the baseline, and BM25+BGE-M3+E5 with BM25+BGE-M3 to assess the contribution of E5. Evaluate fine-tuning by comparing pretrained and fine-tuned BGE-M3 on the same test dataset. Assess standalone and hybrid results separately, keeping BM25, E5, and fusion weights fixed within each hybrid comparison.
\end{enumerate}

The ALQAC and Zalo benchmark averages the evaluation scores of all queries, giving each query equal weight. Each query is evaluated once, using weights selected on other folds. The BCA aggregate describes performance over the 546 retained queries.


Scores are reported to four decimal places, with scientific notation for small nonzero values. Differences are calculated before rounding. Each fine-tuning experiment uses its own pretrained baseline, and results from the two selection procedures are reported separately.

\subsection{BCA Dataset Results}
\label{sec:bca-results}

\subsubsection{Collected Records and Target Coverage}

The collection contains 652 distinct public question-answer records. Of these, 546 contain at least one extracted legal document reference and are retained for retrieval evaluation. The remaining 106 records are excluded because no reference was extracted. These excluded records is still a law question but the answers did not contain any legal references. \subsubsection{Observed Problems in Public-Service Questions}

The collection records two distinct obstacles to constructing retrieval targets. Reference extraction yields no target for the 106 excluded records. These records is still a law question but the answers did not contain any legal references. This is a common problem in public-service questions, where the answer may describe a legal requirement without citing the relevant decree or article. The 229 partially covered queries illustrate another difficulty: the recorded targets are not fully matched to the indexed articles. the answers despite comming from official sources, still have incomplete or incorrect references. Another conclusion is, due to the nature of the BCA dataset, which contains public-service questions with everyday language, the result is sub 60\% NDCG@10, which is lower than the other datasets. This indicates that the BCA dataset is more challenging for retrieval models, likely due to the informal language and incomplete or incorrect references in the questions and answers.

\subsubsection{Summary of Fine-Tuning Results}

The hybrid-selected configuration increases development scores but lowers NDCG@10 for both hybrids in every reported seed. The standalone-selected configuration increases dense NDCG@10 and MRR@10 in every seed but lowers Recall@100. Neither corresponding hybrid reaches the predefined +0.01 NDCG@10 improvement threshold.

These results show that fine-tuning BGE-M3 can be effective for standalone dense retrieval, currently not improving hybrid retrieval performance.

\subsubsection{Highest-Scoring Retrieval Configurations}

On ALQAC, BM25+BGE-M3 has the highest observed NDCG@10, MRR@10, MAP@10, and Hit@10. Adding E5 slightly reduces all four metrics.

Across the evaluated conditions, both hybrids have higher NDCG@10 than BM25. The contribution of E5 varies by condition and metric. Overall, the highest-scoring configuration depends on the metric and dataset. These results do not establish a general E5 improvement.

The first BCA failure concerns permission to use roads with stopping and parking restrictions. The recorded target addresses traffic organization, including the designation of restricted roads and parking locations. Competition between these details is a plausible explanation for the missed target.

The second BCA failure concerns the disposal of seized substances associated with drugs. The target describes the handling of evidence using the legal term \textit{``vật chứng''}, which does not appear in the question. Both hybrids instead retrieve a provision explicitly mentioning drug samples and their disposal. Its scope is the management of samples for defence and security purposes, rather than the general handling of evidence. This case illustrates a possible gap between a public-service question and the legal terminology of its recorded target. The retrieved provision may still contain relevant information; its absence from the labels does not establish that it is legally incorrect.

Both BCA target articles are present in the corpus, so their absence from the top 10 is a ranking failure under the recorded labels. Together, the examples suggest that matching a topic is insufficient when the required procedure or regulatory scope differs. These interpretations explain individual outcomes, not the frequency or confirmed causes of errors across the dataset.

The first case concerns the availability of supervision. The answer reports an administrative process rather than supplying an explicit statutory basis, and the saved extraction contains no reference. Such a record may remain useful as a public question-answer pair, but it does not provide an article-level relevance label for this evaluation. An empty extraction alone is not evidence that every excluded answer lacks citations; extraction failures would require separate inspection.

The second case shows why legal identifiers require more than lowercasing and whitespace removal. The saved document name is incomplete, and visually similar characters occur in the source citation and the corpus identifier. The recorded correction resolves this example, but the extraction remains dependent on an explicit mapping. This is a resolved identifier-alignment problem, rather than evidence of a ranking failure or a verified error in the published legal answer.

The third case concerns the structure of legal evidence. An answer can rely on an appendix item as well as numbered articles in the same decree. The current target schema records document and article identifiers, so the appendix citation is represented only at document level. Under the matching rules in Section~\ref{sec:target-metrics}, any indexed article from that decree may satisfy the whole-document target. Evaluation credit therefore does not demonstrate retrieval of the cited appendix item. Preserving appendix and item identifiers would require extending both corpus extraction and target matching.

The fourth case concerns unresolved document acquisition and mapping. The placeholder records the pipeline's outcome; it does not establish that the decision is confidential, unavailable from every public source, or legally invalid. Its exclusion from corpus additions leaves that recorded target without a document supplied through this mapping. More generally, a missing target match can reflect absent corpus content or an identifier problem, and those causes should be checked before attributing the failure to a retrieval model.

These cases illustrate distinct limits in reference extraction, identifier alignment, target granularity, and corpus construction. They complement the BCA ranking examples, whose target articles are present in the corpus. The examples were selected for inspection and do not measure the frequency of each problem or its contribution to the aggregate BCA scores.

\subsection{Summary of Results}



From the overall benchmark, pretrained BGE-M3 has higher NDCG@10 than BM25 and E5 on both ALQAC and Zalo.

Both hybrids BM25+BGE-M3 and BM25+BGE-M3+E5 have higher NDCG@10 than BM25 and the individual dense retrievers. The contribution of E5 is small and varies by condition.

The finetune version of BGE-M3 does not improve hybrid retrieval in both dataset experiments.
This provides evidence that the pretrained BGE-M3 is already well-suited for hybrid retrieval in the legal domain, and further fine-tuning does not yield significant improvements.

\chapter{Experiments and Evaluation}

This chapter describes the experimental conditions and benchmark evaluation procedure, then presents the results and analysis. The results cover the Bộ Công an (BCA) collection, BGE-M3 fine-tuning relative to matched pretrained baselines, and the overall retrieval benchmark, including the contributions of hybrid retrieval and multilingual-E5-base (E5).

\section{Experimental Conditions}
\label{sec:results-experimental-conditions}

The overall benchmark uses the supplied ALQAC and Zalo reference corpora, while BCA uses its separately constructed corpus. The retrieval models are BM25, \texttt{BAAI/bge-m3}, and \texttt{intfloat/multilingual-e5-base}. Baseline dense inputs are limited to 512 tokens. Dataset sizes, preprocessing, and model settings are specified in Sections~\ref{sec:datasets-corpus} and~\ref{sec:retrieval-implementation}.

\section{Benchmark \& Evaluation Procedure}
\label{sec:benchmark-evaluation-procedure}

The overall benchmark evaluates ALQAC and Zalo using their supplied reference article text. BCA uses the corpus constructed from collected legal documents. Each fine-tuning experiment uses its recorded corpus and compares pretrained and fine-tuned models on the same test queries. The datasets and corpus preparation are described in Sections~\ref{sec:datasets-corpus} and~\ref{sec:bca-construction}.

The evaluation follows six steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Prepare and align the inputs.} Prepare question text, article text, and relevance targets using the procedures in Chapter~4. Check document and article identifiers, and keep the article order consistent across retrievers. Recorded targets are used for selection and evaluation, not as retrieval inputs.
    \item \textbf{Separate selection and evaluation queries.} For the pretrained benchmark, keep questions linked by shared legal references in the same group. For ALQAC and Zalo, select fusion weights on the other folds and evaluate the excluded fold. For the reported BCA comparison, use 62 queries to select weights and all 484 queries in the largest group for evaluation. Fine-tuning uses the training, development, and test allocations in Section~\ref{sec:bge-finetuning}.
    \item \textbf{Compute article scores.} BM25, BGE-M3, and E5 score the same ordered corpus within each comparison. For hybrid retrieval, normalize each retriever's scores separately for each query over the full corpus, then combine them using a weighted sum, as defined in Section~\ref{sec:score-fusion}.
    \item \textbf{Select the configuration.} Choose fusion weights using mean normalized discounted cumulative gain at rank ten (NDCG@10) on the selection queries. For fine-tuning, select the model using either hybrid or standalone dense performance on development data, according to the corresponding procedure. Fix the selected configuration before test evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Evaluate the rankings.} Compare ranked article identifiers with the recorded relevance targets under the rules in Section~\ref{sec:target-metrics}. NDCG@10 is the primary metric. Hit@10 measures whether a target is retrieved within the first ten results, mean reciprocal rank at ten (MRR@10) measures how early the first match occurs, and mean average precision at ten (MAP@10) summarizes precision at matching ranks. The standalone dense and corresponding hybrid comparisons also report Recall@100 using the first 100 results.
    \item \textbf{Compare matched results.} Compare BM25+BGE-M3 with BM25 to assess improvement over the lexical baseline. Compare BM25+BGE-M3+E5 with BM25+BGE-M3 to assess the contribution of E5. Compare fine-tuned with pretrained BGE-M3 on the same test queries, assessing standalone and hybrid performance separately. Within each hybrid fine-tuning comparison, BM25, E5, and fusion weights remain fixed.
\end{enumerate}

ALQAC and Zalo baseline results give each query equal weight and combine its out-of-fold prediction; they do not average fold scores equally when fold sizes differ. The reported BCA benchmark describes one group of 484 questions and does not establish variation across independent legal groups. The fine-tuning test sets had been examined in earlier analyses, so the comparisons remain post-hoc despite their exclusion from the corresponding training and model selection. Statistical analysis and the practical improvement threshold are specified in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-design}.

Values are displayed to four decimal places, with scientific notation for small nonzero values. Differences are calculated before rounding. Each fine-tuning experiment uses its own matched pretrained baseline; results from different selection procedures are not pooled.

The collection records two distinct obstacles to constructing retrieval targets. Reference extraction yields no target for the 106 excluded records. These records is still a law question but the answers did not contain any legal references. This is a common problem in public-service questions, where the answer may describe a legal requirement without citing the relevant decree or article. The 229 partially covered queries illustrate another difficulty: the recorded targets are not fully matched to the indexed articles. the answers despite comming from official sources, still have incomplete or incorrect references. From this result we can say that hybrid retrieval improves the overall retrieval performance on the BCA dataset, with the combination of BM25, BGE-M3, and E5 yielding the best NDCG@10 and Hit@10 scores.

Another conclusion is, due to the nature of the BCA dataset, which contains public-service questions with everyday language, the result is sub 30\% NDCG@10, which is lower than the other datasets. This indicates that the BCA dataset is more challenging for retrieval models, likely due to the informal language and incomplete or incorrect references in the questions and answers.

\subsubsection{Summary of Fine-Tuning Results}

The hybrid-selected configuration increases development scores but lowers test NDCG@10 for both hybrids in every reported seed. The standalone-selected configuration increases dense NDCG@10 and MRR@10 in every seed but lowers Recall@100. Neither corresponding hybrid reaches the predefined +0.01 NDCG@10 improvement threshold.

These post-hoc results show an observed dense ranking gain without a practically useful hybrid gain under the fixed fusion weights. The experiments use different training procedures and test allocations, so their differences cannot be attributed to a single parameter. Retuned fusion weights and transfer to ALQAC or BCA were not evaluated.

\subsubsection{Highest-Scoring Retrieval Configurations}

On ALQAC, BM25+BGE-M3 has the highest observed NDCG@10, MRR@10, MAP@10, and Hit@10. Adding E5 slightly reduces all four metrics.

On Zalo, BM25+BGE-M3+E5 has the highest NDCG@10, MRR@10, and MAP@10, while BM25+BGE-M3 has the highest Hit@10. The small positive NDCG@10 difference from adding E5 is not supported as an improvement by the reported query-level test.

For the 484-query BCA group, BM25+BGE-M3+E5 has the highest observed NDCG@10 and Hit@10, whereas BM25+BGE-M3 has the highest MRR@10 and MAP@10. These descriptive results do not establish an advantage across independent legal groups.

Across the evaluated conditions, both hybrids have higher NDCG@10 than BM25. The contribution of E5 varies by condition and metric. Overall, the highest-scoring configuration depends on the metric and dataset. These results do not establish a general E5 improvement.

\subsection{Summary of Results}



From the overall benchmark, pretrained BGE-M3 has higher NDCG@10 than BM25 and E5 on both ALQAC and Zalo.

Both hybrids BM25+BGE-M3 and BM25+BGE-M3+E5 have higher NDCG@10 than BM25 and the individual dense retrievers. The contribution of E5 is small and varies by condition.

The finetune version of BGE-M3 does not improve hybrid retrieval in both dataset experiments.
This provides evidence that the pretrained BGE-M3 is already well-suited for hybrid retrieval in the legal domain, and further fine-tuning does not yield significant improvements.
